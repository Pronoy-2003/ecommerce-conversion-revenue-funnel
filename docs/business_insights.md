# Business Insights

## Executive Summary

The analysis of October 2019 e-commerce behavior shows that the largest opportunity is not simply generating more traffic. The business already receives substantial product-view activity.

The primary opportunity is to **convert existing traffic more effectively**, particularly at the View → Cart stage, while prioritizing segments based on conversion, scale, and revenue contribution.

## Key Findings

### 1. View → Cart is the primary funnel bottleneck

Only **11.14%** of viewed users add a product to cart, compared with **58.35%** Cart → Purchase conversion.

**Implication:** Improving the product-view-to-cart stage should be a major conversion priority.

### 2. Smartphones are the strongest category

`electronics.smartphone` generated approximately **$157.0M** in revenue, with about **1.30M viewing users** and **9.25% View → Purchase** conversion.

**Implication:** This category combines scale, conversion, and revenue, making it important to protect and study for successful merchandising practices.

### 3. High traffic does not always mean strong conversion

Several categories receive substantial viewing traffic but have weaker View → Purchase conversion than stronger-performing categories.

**Implication:** High-traffic, low-conversion categories should be prioritized because small improvements can affect a large customer base.

### 4. Revenue and conversion tell different stories

The **$100–$250** price band has the highest View → Purchase conversion at approximately **5.06%**.

The **$500–$1,000** and **$1,000–$2,000** bands generate approximately **$62.9M** and **$62.0M** respectively.

**Implication:** Optimization should consider both conversion efficiency and revenue contribution rather than focusing on either metric alone.

### 5. $2,000+ products have very low conversion

The **$2,000+** price band has approximately **0.24% View → Purchase** conversion.

**Implication:** This segment warrants investigation into pricing, trust, product information, payment flexibility, or other purchase-friction factors. These are hypotheses, not proven causes from this dataset.

### 6. Revenue is concentrated among leading brands

Apple generated approximately **$111.2M**, followed by Samsung at approximately **$46.4M** and Xiaomi at approximately **$9.2M**.

**Implication:** Leading brands are important revenue drivers and should receive appropriate merchandising and inventory attention.

### 7. Conversion and revenue peak at different times

Tuesday has the strongest day-level first-view cohort View → Purchase conversion at approximately **7.29%**.

Wednesday generates the highest actual purchase revenue at approximately **$37.06M**.

Around **09:00 UTC** has the strongest first-view cohort conversion at approximately **7.71%** and the highest hourly purchase revenue at approximately **$17.55M**.

**Implication:** Campaign and operational planning should consider both conversion efficiency and revenue volume.

# Priority Recommendations

1. **Improve View → Cart conversion** through product-page and add-to-cart experience optimization.
2. **Prioritize high-traffic, low-conversion categories** for targeted investigation and experimentation.
3. **Protect high-performing categories and brands** while studying practices associated with stronger conversion.
4. **Investigate high-price conversion barriers**, especially in the $2,000+ segment.
5. **Use time-based patterns** to support campaign timing and operational planning.

## Overall Conclusion

The largest opportunity is not simply generating more traffic. The business already receives substantial product-view activity.

The stronger opportunity is to **convert existing traffic more effectively**, particularly at the View → Cart stage, while prioritizing high-traffic segments and considering conversion, scale, and revenue together.
